# Behavioral Phenotyping

## Purpose

The 60-family confirmatory experiment did not support the broad hypothesis that generic formal register systematically increases model agreement with false claims.

However, the confirmatory effects were highly heterogeneous across individual stimuli.

This exploratory analysis therefore asked whether particular linguistic realizations of register were associated with larger positive or negative changes in model agreement.

The goal was not to rescue the original hypothesis, but to identify a narrower candidate phenomenon that could subsequently be tested on completely new held-out stimuli.

---

## Starting Point: Confirmatory Null

The confirmatory experiment used 60 factual families with four conditions:

- true plain
- true formal
- false plain
- false formal

The mean false-claim register effect was:

`false_formal - false_plain = +0.3376`

The mean true-claim register effect was:

`true_formal - true_plain = +0.0179`

The false-minus-true interaction was:

`+0.3197`

The primary interaction was not statistically reliable:

`t(59) = 0.7996, p = .4272`

This provided no evidence for a general formal-register deference effect.

---

## Why Phenotype the Stimuli?

The original 20-item pilot effect survived correction of the Gemma response interface, yet the stronger 60-family confirmatory experiment did not reproduce the broad effect.

This suggested that the pilot result might depend on the particular way that "formal" variants were linguistically realized.

The exploratory question therefore became:

> Which properties of the formal transformations are associated with larger shifts in model agreement?

---

## Register-Realization Coding

Each of the 60 plain/formal transformations was coded using a pre-specified linguistic coding manual.

The coded dimensions were:

- technical lexicon
- role/status framing
- taxonomic/categorical framing
- relation reframing
- syntactic restructuring
- nominalization
- semantic specificity change

The final coding should be described as:

**assisted exploratory linguistic coding using a pre-specified coding manual**

It should not be described as fully independently blinded coding.

---

## Descriptive Results

For false claims, the mean difference between items where each feature was present versus absent was:

| Feature | Mean difference |
|---|---:|
| Role/status framing | +2.465 |
| Nominalization | +1.778 |
| Taxonomic/categorical framing | +1.312 |
| Relation reframing | +0.727 |
| Technical lexicon | -0.070 |
| Syntactic restructuring | -0.784 |

Technical vocabulary therefore provided essentially no explanation for the observed heterogeneity.

The strongest descriptive association was role/status framing.

---

## Feature Overlap

The coded features were not independent.

In particular:

- role/status framing co-occurred with relation reframing in 6 of 8 role/status cases;
- relation reframing and syntactic restructuring were strongly associated;
- nominalization occurred in only 4 cases.

This made it inappropriate to interpret the individual feature means as independent causal effects.

The strongest combined pattern was therefore examined directly.

---

## Role-Relational Candidate

A candidate group was defined by the joint presence of:

1. role/status framing; and
2. relation reframing.

Examples of the relevant constructions included:

- `serves as`
- `functions as`
- `qualifies as`
- `ranks as`
- `is the author of`

For the six role-relational cases:

- n = 6
- mean false-claim effect = +3.145
- median = +1.412
- positive rate = 6/6

For the remaining 54 cases:

- mean = +0.026
- median = +0.143
- positive rate = 53.7%

The candidate-minus-rest mean difference was:

`+3.119`

---

## Robustness Checks

### Leave-one-out

Removing any single role-relational item left all remaining effects positive.

### Removing extreme effects

With all six cases:

- mean = +3.145
- 6/6 positive

After removing the single largest effect:

- mean = +2.038
- 5/5 positive

After removing the two largest effects:

- mean = +0.840
- 4/4 positive

### Construction-cluster check

Three role-relational examples involved capital-city propositions.

Capital-city cases:

- n = 3
- mean = +3.217
- positive rate = 3/3

Other role-relational cases:

- n = 3
- mean = +3.072
- positive rate = 3/3

The pattern was therefore not restricted to the capital-city construction cluster.

---

## Updated Candidate Phenomenon

The exploratory evidence suggests that the relevant phenomenon may not be generic formality.

A more specific candidate is:

### Role-relational framing

Role-relational framing occurs when a proposition is expressed through a predicate that construes an entity in terms of a role, function, status, qualification, ranking, authorship, or analogous relation rather than through simpler direct predication.

The current working hypothesis is:

> Role-relational framing may increase model agreement with false propositions relative to matched direct or merely formal formulations.

This hypothesis remains exploratory.

---

## What Is Not Supported

The present evidence does not support the claim that:

- formal language generally causes epistemic deference;
- technical vocabulary alone causes epistemic deference;
- the model has already been shown to represent speaker authority;
- an authority or indexical circuit has been identified;
- role-relational framing has been causally established.

---

## Next Experimental Gate

The role-relational candidate must now be tested on a completely new held-out stimulus bank.

The next experiment will separate:

1. plain direct predication
2. formal lexical control
3. role-relational framing

The critical comparison will be:

`false_role_relational - false_formal_control`

Mechanistic analysis will begin only if the held-out behavioral effect replicates under the pre-specified design.

# Stimulus Validation Plan

## Purpose

The behavioral pilot suggested that formal-register reformulations may increase model agreement with false factual claims. Before expanding the experiment or beginning mechanistic analysis, the linguistic stimuli will be independently validated.

The purpose of validation is to establish that the plain and formal variants differ primarily in linguistic register while remaining closely matched in propositional meaning and other potentially confounding properties.

## Materials

The initial validation set consists of the 20 matched plain/formal pairs used in the behavioral pilot:

- 10 false factual claims
- 10 true factual claims
- 20 plain-register sentences
- 20 formal-register sentences
- 40 sentences in total

The original pilot stimuli will remain unchanged. Validation results will be used to design a new confirmatory stimulus set rather than retrospectively modifying the pilot.

## Human raters

Target: at least 10 independent adult raters who are proficient speakers of English and are not informed of the experimental hypothesis.

If the ratings are intended for publication or formal research dissemination, applicable institutional research-ethics requirements will be checked before recruitment.

## Sentence-level ratings

Each sentence will be rated independently on 7-point scales.

### Formality

1 = very informal  
7 = very formal

This is the primary manipulation check.

### Assertiveness / confidence

1 = very tentative or uncertain  
7 = very assertive or confident

Plain and formal variants should remain closely matched on this dimension.

### Politeness

1 = very impolite  
7 = very polite

Plain and formal variants should remain closely matched on this dimension.

### Naturalness

1 = very unnatural English  
7 = completely natural English

Both versions should be reasonably natural.

### Perceived speaker expertise / authority

1 = speaker sounds very inexpert / low authority  
7 = speaker sounds highly expert / authoritative

This dimension is measured as a theoretically relevant potential mediator rather than as an exclusion criterion.

### Perceived evidential strength

1 = provides essentially no evidential support  
7 = provides very strong evidential support

Plain and formal versions should remain closely matched on this dimension.

## Pair-level semantic validation

Each matched plain/formal pair will also be evaluated for semantic equivalence.

### Semantic equivalence

1 = substantially different claims  
7 = essentially the same factual claim

Raters will also answer:

"Do these two sentences make the same factual proposition?"

Response options:

- Yes
- No
- Unsure

## Presentation

For sentence-level ratings:

- sentences will be presented individually;
- plain/formal labels will not be shown;
- truth status will not be shown;
- sentences will be randomized;
- matched versions should not deliberately appear next to one another.

For semantic-equivalence ratings:

- matched sentences will be presented together;
- left/right order will be randomized;
- register labels and truth status will not be displayed.

## Pre-specified screening criteria

A stimulus pair will be considered suitable for the confirmatory stimulus bank when it approximately meets the following criteria:

1. Formality: mean formal-rating score at least 1.0 point higher than the matched plain score.
2. Semantic equivalence: mean rating of at least 6.0 on the 7-point scale.
3. Assertiveness/confidence: absolute plain-formal difference no greater than 0.75 points.
4. Politeness: absolute plain-formal difference no greater than 0.75 points.
5. Evidential strength: absolute plain-formal difference no greater than 0.75 points.
6. Naturalness: mean rating of at least 4.5 for both variants.

Perceived speaker expertise/authority will be analysed but will not be used as an exclusion criterion because increased perceived authority is a hypothesized pathway through which register may influence epistemic deference.

These thresholds are screening rules for stimulus development rather than inferential statistical tests.

## Analysis plan

For every stimulus pair, the following will be calculated:

- mean plain formality
- mean formal formality
- formal-minus-plain formality difference
- mean semantic-equivalence rating
- plain/formal assertiveness difference
- plain/formal politeness difference
- plain/formal evidential-strength difference
- plain/formal naturalness
- plain/formal perceived-authority difference

Pairs failing validation will not be deleted from the historical pilot. Instead, they will be revised or replaced when constructing the confirmatory stimulus set.

## Next-stage confirmatory design

After validation, a larger stimulus bank will be created using matched factual families.

Where possible, each factual family will include:

- true + plain register
- true + formal register
- false + plain register
- false + formal register

This structure will allow truth status and register to be manipulated within the same factual domain rather than comparing unrelated true and false propositions.

The confirmatory behavioral experiment will continue to counterbalance the A/B response-label order.

A portion of the validated stimulus families will be held out from exploratory mechanistic analysis and reserved for later generalization tests.
